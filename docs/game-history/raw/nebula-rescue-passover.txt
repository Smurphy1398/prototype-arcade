Current problems to fix:

1. Lower playfield / flipper area is still messy

The bottom third of the table needs the most work. It should look and feel like a real pinball control zone.

Problems:
- Slingshots are too horizontal and awkward.
- Slings crowd the top of the flippers and block the cradle/trap area.
- There are tiny dot/post/ghost-hit objects above or near the slings/flippers that make the ball hit things the player cannot understand.
- The lower playfield has too many leftover rails, lights, guides, and clutter.
- Some objects appear to clip/intersect.
- The ball sometimes reacts to things that are visually unclear.

Fix:
- Rebuild/clean the lower third as one coherent package.
- Slingshots should be diagonal, readable, and placed above/outside the flippers.
- Slings should not block most of the top of the flipper.
- Remove or disable leftover ghost posts/dots/hidden helper collisions.
- Every active collider must have a visible reason to exist.
- No invisible bumpers over the flippers.
- No random small post/dot collisions in the lower control zone.
- Keep only essential lower-lane guides: inlanes, outlanes, kickback, extra ball, drain, flippers, slings.

2. Flipper feel needs improvement

Current flippers are playable but still not satisfying.

Problems:
- They do not hit hard enough.
- The ball is hard to finesse or juggle lightly.
- Holding the flipper should allow trapping/cradling, but currently the ball tends to roll off the back or get knocked unpredictably.
- Held flippers should not act like a perfect full-power shot every time.
- The player should be able to do light taps, controlled shots, and catch/trap behavior.

Fix:
- Increase initial snap/power on fresh flipper presses.
- Preserve quick-tap responsiveness.
- Holding a flipper should create a raised-bat/trap behavior, not a constant auto-launch.
- Improve cradle/trap behavior so the ball can rest/control better on a held flipper.
- Prevent easy roll-off behind the flipper.
- Do not rewrite flipper physics from scratch unless absolutely necessary; tune the existing system carefully.

Desired feel:
- Tap = sharp flick.
- Hold = control/trap.
- Release/re-hit = finesse shot.
- Fresh rising stroke = strong shot.
- Already-held flipper contact = soft control, not full cannon launch.

3. Spinner/flapper targets need physics scaling

Problems:
- Spinner/flapper doors can feel too fast compared with the rest of the game physics.
- They should not spin like detached fans.
- They should respond to ball speed but remain believable.

Fix:
- Cap max spinner angular velocity.
- Ball speed should determine spin torque.
- Slow ball = small flap/spin.
- Fast ball = fast spin.
- Add damping so it spins down naturally.
- Avoid constant sound spam.

4. Lighting needs visible sources and coherent sequencing

The shooter runway lights are the best-looking lighting feature so far. Use that as the model.

Problems:
- Other board lights still feel like floating/no-source glow.
- There are random glows/lights with unclear purpose.
- Lighting is noisy instead of dramatic.

Fix:
- Every light should belong to a visible object: insert, lamp, bumper ring, sling insert, ramp arrow, wormhole ring, mission core insert, kickback/extra ball insert, or runway lamp.
- Reduce or remove mystery point lights.
- Keep the shooter runway chase lights.
- Add coherent light sequences to other zones without clutter.

Needed light groups:
- Fuel bank inserts: cyan/green.
- Mission core inserts: blue/yellow.
- Moon ramp arrows: amber.
- Wormhole ring: purple/magenta.
- Bumper field: warm orange/white bumper rings.
- Slings: small embedded sling lamps.
- Kickback / extra ball: low-profile inserts.
- Runway: existing chase lights.

Lighting states to preserve/improve:
- Attract mode.
- Ball ready / launch bay armed.
- Launch runway chase.
- Standard gameplay objective pulse.
- Local shot effects.
- Mission ready / complete.
- Meteor / wormhole / supernova skybox reactions.
- Ball save / kickback / extra ball.
