Tutorial / Rulebook

Goal: In chess, checkmate the enemy king. In checkers, capture or trap all opposing pieces. Click a piece, then click a highlighted legal square.

Chess piece basics

Pawn: moves forward 1, or 2 from its starting square, and captures diagonally. Rook: straight lines. Bishop: diagonals. Knight: L-shape and can jump over pieces. Queen: rook + bishop movement. King: one square any direction, but may never move into check.

Chess special rules

Check: your king is under attack and you must escape. Checkmate: king is in check and there is no legal escape. Stalemate: king is not in check, but that player has no legal move, so it is a draw. This often happens when a king is boxed in but not directly attacked. Castling: king moves two squares toward an unmoved rook and the rook jumps beside it, only if neither piece moved, the path is clear, and the king does not move through check. Promotion: a pawn reaching the far side becomes queen, rook, bishop, or knight. En passant: immediately after an enemy pawn moves two squares beside your pawn, your pawn may capture it diagonally as if it moved only one square.

Strategy basics

Protect your queen and rooks, develop knights/bishops early, fight for the center, keep your king safe, and do not trade a valuable piece for a cheaper one unless there is a tactic. Piece value guide: pawn 1, knight/bishop 3, rook 5, queen 9.

Checkers rules

Men move diagonally forward. Kings move diagonally forward or backward. A jump captures an adjacent enemy if the square behind it is empty. Official rules require a jump when available, but Casual mode makes jumps optional. Multiple jumps continue from the landing square. Repeated positions or too many no-progress moves can end as a draw.

Controls

Use Lock Camera while playing to avoid nudging the view. Use the Orbit Pad or camera presets to adjust the board. Use Hide Icons for a cleaner 3D look, or Show Icons for easier learning.
